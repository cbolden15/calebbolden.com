This one takes apart a single workflow end to end: intake, quote, follow-up. It's the most common shape I see across service businesses, so it's a useful one to start with.

Intake. A lead comes in (call, form, referral) and the details land somewhere: a notebook, a shared inbox, a spreadsheet tab nobody else opens. The manual step here is almost always the same one: someone has to notice the lead exists before anything happens to it. If that someone is on vacation or slammed, the lead sits.

Quote. Once the lead is noticed, building the quote usually means pulling numbers from two or three places (a pricing sheet, a materials list, maybe a past job for reference) and assembling them by hand into whatever document or email goes out. Every extra minute here is a minute the customer is also getting quotes from someone else.

Follow-up. The quote goes out and then, in most shops, nothing happens automatically. Somebody has to remember to check back in three days, and that somebody is usually the busiest person in the building. Follow-up is where deals that were genuinely winnable quietly die from neglect, not from losing on price.

The fix, in outline: intake gets logged automatically the moment it lands, the quote pulls from one source of pricing truth instead of three, and follow-up fires on a schedule without depending on someone's memory. None of the three steps needs a person doing something a system can do instead. They need a person doing the parts a system can't: judgment calls, relationship, the actual work.
