A field note this time: what a text-back actually recovers, and what it doesn't.

A text-back is the simplest fix in this whole category. Somebody calls, nobody picks up, and within a minute or two they get a text: sorry we missed you, here's how to book, or reply here and we'll call you right back. It's not answering the phone. It's catching the person before they've already dialed the next name on the list.

What it recovers is the customer who was already trying to give you the job and just hit a dead end. Most people who call a service business aren't shopping five vendors: they found one number, called it, and expected someone on the other end. A text-back inside two minutes puts a human back in that conversation before the moment passes. It doesn't fix a bad estimate, a slow no-show, or a job that goes sideways once your truck shows up. It only closes the gap between the phone ringing and someone actually responding.

What it can't do: it won't rescue a caller who wanted a live person right now and got a text instead. Some of those calls are gone no matter what. And it's only as good as what happens after the reply; if the follow-through is slow, the text just delays the loss by a few hours.
