You're in. This is The Missed Call, and this first note is the math I use every time I sit down with a service business owner, so you have it too.

Here's a plain version of it. Say a two-truck plumbing outfit books 15 jobs a week off inbound calls, at $380 average ticket. That's roughly $5,700 a week from calls that got answered. Now count the calls that didn't. Most shops running a lean crew miss somewhere between 20% and 35% of inbound calls during a normal week: nights, weekends, mid-job, lunch. If that shop is missing 25% of, say, 20 total inbound calls, that's 5 calls a week nobody picked up.

Not all 5 would have booked. Call it a 50% close rate on answered calls, generously. Still: 2 to 3 jobs a week, every week, going to whichever competitor answered instead. At that same $380 average ticket, that's $800 to $1,100 a week in work that already called and already wanted the job.

That number isn't from a case study. It's arithmetic you can run against your own call log this afternoon: count the calls, count the answered ones, count the booked ones, and see what the gap costs at your own average ticket. Most owners have never actually done that count. Worth ten minutes.
