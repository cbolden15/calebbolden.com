Somewhere in your business right now, someone is retyping a number that already exists in another system. Or waiting on an approval that's stuck in a Slack thread because the tool that's supposed to route it doesn't talk to the other tool. Or manually checking three places to answer a question one dashboard should already answer. None of that shows up as a line item anywhere. There's no expense category called "copying data between spreadsheets." It just shows up as hours that were supposed to go somewhere else and didn't.

Call it the manual-work tax: the fixed cost of every gap between two systems that don't share data, paid every single week whether or not the business grows. It doesn't scale down when things are slow, and it gets worse, not better, as headcount grows, since more people means more numbers getting copied into more places. Most ops leaders can name their biggest one in under ten seconds when asked directly. Almost none of them have written it down or put a number on it.
