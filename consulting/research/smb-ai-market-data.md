# SMB AI market data — verified stats for workshops, lead magnet, and sales conversations

Collected 2026-07-06. Only figures traceable to named primary surveys are listed. Adoption numbers vary wildly by methodology: rigorous government surveys (Census BTOS) measure ~18% of all US firms using AI, while self-report industry surveys run 46-88% depending on how "using AI" is defined. Quote the source with the stat.

## The gap that sells assessment-first consulting

- Only 14% of small businesses are "fully integrating AI into core operations" despite 76% using it somewhere (Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses, n=1,256, Feb 2026)
- Of AI-using small firms, ~50% are "experimenting," 44% "partially integrated," only 7% "fully integrated" (Federal Reserve Small Business Credit Survey, n=6,525 employer firms, late 2025)
- 95% of GenAI pilots show no P&L impact; causes are integration, data, and workflow gaps, not model quality (Project NANDA, MIT Media Lab, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025." Caveat before quoting: not peer-reviewed, methodology disputed, and NANDA sells an agentic-AI vision. Related stat from the same report: external partnerships succeed ~67% of the time vs ~33% for internal builds)
- ~40% of SMB leaders haven't seen measurable AI results yet; top unmet needs are expertise, data readiness, and a clear starting point (ECI Software Solutions AI Readiness Report, n=550+, Nov 2025)
- 74% of not-yet-adopters would adopt with clearer ROI evidence; practical training is the top support need (PayPal/Reimagine Main Street, n≈1,000, June 2025)
- 73% of small business owners say more training/resources would help them implement AI successfully (Goldman Sachs, Feb 2026)

## AI project failure stats (verified, safe to quote with attribution)

These come from firms SMB owners' advisors recognize and are sturdier than the NANDA 95% number:

- Gartner (Jul 2024): 30% of GenAI projects abandoned after proof of concept by end of 2025 — poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs, unclear business value
- Gartner (Feb 2025): 60% of AI projects abandoned through 2026 for lack of AI-ready data; 63% of orgs lack the data management practices AI needs
- Gartner (Jun 2025): over 40% of agentic AI projects predicted canceled by end of 2027; only ~130 of thousands of "agentic" vendors are real ("agent washing")
- BCG (Oct 2024, n=1,000 executives): 74% of companies have yet to show tangible value from AI; only 4% generate significant value
- McKinsey State of AI (Nov 2025, n=1,993): 88% of orgs use AI somewhere, only ~7% fully scaled; only 39% report any enterprise EBIT impact
- S&P Global 451 Research (Mar 2025, n=1,006): share of companies abandoning most AI initiatives jumped 17% → 42% in one year; average org scrapped 46% of AI POCs before production
- RAND (2024, 65 expert interviews): "by some estimates" 80%+ of AI projects fail — top root cause is leadership miscommunicating the problem to solve (84% of interviewees), then data quality, tech-chasing over problem-solving, deployment underinvestment
- BCG (Sept 2025, n=1,250): 60% of companies generate no material value from AI despite continued investment; only 5% create substantial value at scale. BCG's stated success ratio: 70% people/process, 20% technology, 10% algorithms — the single best workshop soundbite for process-first positioning
- "Pilot purgatory" as a term: WEF/McKinsey manufacturing work, 2018 — useful vocabulary for workshops

RAND's #1 root cause (leaders miscommunicate what problem to solve) is the strongest single citation for the lean positioning: problem definition is the failure point, and problem definition is the product.

No rigorous SMB-specific AI failure-rate research exists — every hard failure percentage above comes from enterprise-skewed samples. Never quote an "SMB AI failure rate"; frame as "here's what the enterprise data shows, and SMBs have fewer resources to absorb these mistakes."

## Adoption by size band (your 10-200 employee target)

- NFIB members: 21% adoption at single-digit-employee firms rising to 48% at 50+ employees (NFIB Small Business and Technology Survey, n=521, June 2025)
- Census BTOS (Dec 2025-May 2026): 100-249 employees at 32%, 250+ at 37%, all-business average 17-20%. Growth concentrated in firms with 20+ employees
- OECD (2024): 10-49 employees 11.9%, 50-249 employees 20.4%, 250+ 40%
- Small/large adoption gap narrowed from 1.8x to near parity between Feb 2024 and Aug 2025 (SBA Office of Advocacy on Census BTOS)

Read: the target band adopts faster than micro-businesses but trails enterprise; most adoption is shallow (chat tools, marketing content), which is where process-first consulting fits.

## What they use AI for (top use cases)

- QuickBooks (Apr 2025): marketing 43%, customer service 36%, admin tasks 33%, data processing 32%, bookkeeping 29%. Agentic AI at only 9%
- Fed SBCS: writing/marketing 83%, individual productivity 61%, planning/analysis 51%
- Typical small business uses a median of 5 AI tools (SBE Council, Mar 2026)

## Barriers (workshop framing)

- Data privacy/security 50%, lack of technical expertise 49%, difficulty choosing tools 48% (Goldman Sachs, Feb 2026)
- Top challenges for existing users: accuracy 46%, adapting tools to business needs 43% (Fed SBCS)
- Only 31% feel prepared for AI regulation compliance (US Chamber, Aug 2025)

## Spend signals

- 93% of AI-using small businesses plan continued investment; 62% expect to increase spend (Goldman Sachs, Feb 2026)
- 78% of growing SMBs plan to increase AI investment vs 55% of declining ones (Salesforce SMB Trends, 6th ed., 2025)

## Do not use

These circulate on marketing blogs but could not be traced to any primary source: "$18,000/yr average SMB AI spend," "$2,068/employee (Atlanta Fed)," "$2,400/yr subscriptions," "12% SMB Copilot adoption," "67.6% of ChatGPT companies are 1-10 employees." McKinsey/Deloitte/Gartner AI surveys are enterprise-skewed, not SMB data.
