# Discovery call script

This is the first call with a prospect, 20-30 minutes, before any paid engagement. It is separate from `discovery-interview-guide.md`, which is the 45-60 minute facilitator script used during the Discover week of a paid audit, run across 5-6 people at the client's business. This call decides whether that engagement happens at all.

Run this call live, on video or phone. Don't send it as a form.

## Before the call

Confirm the prospect passed `lead-qualification-checklist.md`. If they didn't, and you're on the call anyway, treat this as a qualification call, not a sales call, and be ready to end it gracefully (see "Ending well" below).

## Structure: CLOSING, with a why-conversation running underneath

The call follows Comfort, Learn, Obstacles, Stakes, Introduce, Narrow, Get paid. Underneath every question, keep asking why. The first answer someone gives is almost always the symptom, not the problem. "We need better scheduling software" is a symptom. Two or three whys down, the real problem is usually something like "we lose a job every time the owner is the only person who can quote it." Sell against the real problem, not the symptom.

### Comfort (2-3 min)

Open casual, not scripted. Ask how their week is going, mention something specific from research (a review they got, something on their site, a detail from the referral who connected you). The goal is to lower the temperature before asking anything real.

### Learn: open with a diagnostic question (5-8 min)

Ask directly: "What's the biggest thing blocking your customer growth right now?"

Then stop talking. Let them answer fully. Don't interrupt, don't pitch, don't nod along and jump to a solution. Take notes on the exact words they use, you'll reuse their language later in the proposal.

Follow every answer with a why-question, three levels deep if you can get there without it feeling like an interrogation:

- "What makes that the biggest one, versus everything else on the list?"
- "Why does that keep happening, do you think?"
- "What have you already tried on that, and what happened?"

If they name a tool problem ("our CRM is bad"), ask what breaks when the tool fails. That's usually where the real cost lives.

### Obstacles (3-5 min)

Ask what's stopped them from fixing this already. Budget, time, a past bad experience, not knowing where to start, nobody internally who owns it. Write down the actual obstacle in their words, you'll need to address it specifically later, not generically.

### Stakes (3-5 min)

Ask what it costs them to leave this unfixed for another six months. A missed job, a burned-out employee, a customer who left. If they haven't thought about it in dollars, ask them to estimate. Don't supply the number for them, let them say it.

### Introduce (3-5 min)

Now, and only now, describe how you work: map the actual workflow before touching any tool, find where AI helps and where it's a distraction, fixed-price engagements, no long-term retainer required to start. Keep this short. You're not pitching features, you're connecting what you just heard to how you work.

### Narrow: the 1-10 readiness close (3-5 min)

Ask: "On a scale of 1 to 10, how ready are you to move on this?"

Whatever number they give, ask: "What's stopping a 10?"

The answer to that second question is the real objection. It might be price, timing, a partner who needs to sign off, or genuine uncertainty about whether this will work. Treat it as information, not a hurdle to argue past. Log it in `objection-notes.md` after the call regardless of outcome.

### Get paid: the direct-price answer (2-3 min)

When they ask what it costs (and they will, sometimes earlier than this point), answer directly. State the number. Don't deflect into "it depends" or "let's talk about scope first." A direct price answer builds trust; a dodge reads as a sales tactic.

If they ask before you've reached this point in the call, answer it there and then come back to wherever you were. Don't make them ask twice.

Close with a specific next step: book the paid engagement, or set a specific follow-up date if they need to think. Never leave it as "let me know."

## Ending well: the qualification exit

Some calls surface a genuine non-fit: no real budget, no decision-making authority, a problem that needs a different kind of help entirely. End it cleanly.

"Based on what you've told me, I don't think this is the right fit right now, and I'd rather tell you that than take your money for something that won't move the needle. [Optional: here's what I'd look at instead.]"

This isn't a loss. A clean no-fit call is the fastest way to earn a referral later, even from someone who never becomes a client.

## After the call

Log the call in the CRM. Note the readiness number, the stated obstacle, and any objection heard, in `objection-notes.md`. If they're moving forward, send the proposal within 24 hours while the conversation is still fresh for them.
