### Link strategy

One link, not a link-in-bio service. The link field goes to:

```
https://calebbolden.com/tools/ai-readiness
```

Instagram now allows several links, and the temptation is to add the homepage, the newsletter, and a booking page. Don't. One destination is what makes the metric readable: assessment completions per week is the scorecard line, and a second link only splits it. The bio's last line ("Score your business in 10 min:") is written to point straight down at the link, so the two read as one sentence.

Set the profile to a Business or Creator account during setup so the Vora social layer can connect it and pull analytics.

## Profile picture

One photo, used unchanged on all four platforms. Recognition across feeds is worth more than a platform-specific crop, and a person who sees the same face on a Reel and then on LinkedIn should not have to work out that it's the same guy. Shoot it in daylight against a plain light wall, ideally the working wall itself with the map far enough out of focus to read as texture rather than content. Frame head and shoulders, face filling roughly 60% of the square so it survives a 40-pixel avatar, eyes on the lens, a real expression rather than a held smile. Wear a solid mid-tone shirt in a color that isn't blue, so the blueprint-blue in the banners stays the only strong color in the frame. No dark background, no rim light, no desk-with-monitors composition, no purple or violet anywhere including reflections. The photo should look like a daylight photo of a person at work, which is the same promise the rest of the brand makes.

Export one 1:1 master at the largest size the camera gives, then let each platform crop. Check the result as a circle, because three of the four render it that way.

## Banners

Three platforms have banners: LinkedIn, YouTube, Facebook. All three use the same visual language: white mat board, blueprint-blue instrument marks, pencil-gray hairlines, and exactly one amber sticky note. Graph grid at low opacity in the margins only. Archivo for any display text, Martian Mono uppercase for small labels. Nothing gets a dark background and nothing gets a glow. Confirm current pixel dimensions and safe-crop areas in each platform's uploader at design time rather than trusting a spec sheet.

**LinkedIn.** Wide and short, and the profile photo covers the lower left, so everything lives right of center. A single value stream map runs left to right across the band: four blue process boxes joined by dimension lines, with small mono labels under each. The third box has the amber sticky stuck to its corner reading `AUTOMATE THIS`. Right edge carries the wordmark `CALEB BOLDEN` in Archivo and `calebbolden.com/tools/ai-readiness` in mono beneath it. Keep the map's leftmost box behind where the avatar sits, so the composition survives the overlap.

**YouTube.** The banner crops brutally: phones and TVs show wildly different amounts of it, and only the center strip is guaranteed. Put everything that must be read inside that center strip: the wordmark in Archivo, one line under it reading `Process first, then AI` in mono, and the amber sticky offset just right of the wordmark. Fill the outer thirds with blueprint texture alone, faint graph grid and a couple of dotted leader lines running off the edges, so a wide crop looks intentional and a narrow crop loses nothing.

**Facebook.** The Page cover crops differently on desktop and mobile, and mobile takes a tall center slice. Same map motif as LinkedIn but simplified to two process boxes and one dimension line annotating a real number, with `Dallas-Fort Worth` set in mono under the wordmark to make the local claim visible without a sentence. Sticky note goes center-right where both crops keep it.

## Reusable calls to action

All five end at the assessment. Rotate them so the same line doesn't appear under every clip. Use plain text, no emoji, no hashtag stack.

1. `Take the AI readiness assessment: calebbolden.com/tools/ai-readiness` (68 characters)
2. `Eighteen questions, ten minutes, a scored answer on whether you're ready: calebbolden.com/tools/ai-readiness` (108)
3. `Want to know which of these your business already passes? Score it at calebbolden.com/tools/ai-readiness` (104)
4. `Before you buy anything, find out where you actually stand: calebbolden.com/tools/ai-readiness` (94)
5. `Free, no call required: calebbolden.com/tools/ai-readiness` (58)

Lines 1 and 5 fit anywhere, including a spoken sign-off. Line 3 is the one to use under a clip that answers a specific readiness question, because it points back at the question just asked.

### Hashtags

Zero to two, and only where the platform expects them. Instagram and Facebook Reels tolerate two; LinkedIn does not need any and YouTube uses them for nothing useful. When two are used, make them the specific ones (`#smallbusinessowner` plus a trade tag like `#hvac`) rather than the broad ones. A stack of fifteen reads as someone who buys reach instead of earning it, which is the opposite of the pitch.

## Handle

Preferred `@calebbolden`, fallback `@calebboldenai`. Availability is unverified and cannot be checked from here; the founder checks all four platforms at signup before creating anything.

The rule from the setup runbook holds: check all four first, and if the preferred handle is taken on even one platform, use the fallback on all four. A matching handle across four platforms is worth more than winning the better handle on three and mismatching on the fourth, because the handle is what people type when they go looking for the account they saw somewhere else.

Display name stays `Caleb Bolden` on every platform regardless of which handle wins. Record the handle actually used in `consulting/social/account-setup.md`.
