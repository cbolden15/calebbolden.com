# Positioning options

Three options for the one-sentence positioning statement that everything downstream quotes:
homepage, LinkedIn headline, Google Business Profile description, email signature, proposal
opener, cold-email templates. Pick one, or edit one, by Thursday 8/13. Whatever gets picked
replaces the placeholder framing across the site verbatim, not paraphrased per page.

Each option passes the "I help X achieve Y by doing Z" test. Each is grounded in claims the
site already makes (the Assured AI method, the audit-first package structure, the map/gate/
check guarantees on `/method`). No new proof points, numbers, or claims are introduced here.

---

## Option 1: Process discipline first

**Positioning statement:**
I help Fort Worth-area small business owners find out exactly where AI pays off by mapping
their real workflow first, using the same lean process-improvement discipline I've run in
enterprise financial services for twelve years.

**LinkedIn headline** (166 chars):
AI consultant for Fort Worth small businesses. I map your real process before I touch AI,
using 12 years of lean process-improvement discipline. Audits start at $750.

**Google Business Profile description** (614 chars):
I help Fort Worth-area small business owners find out exactly where AI pays off, before they
spend a dollar building it. Every engagement starts with a paid audit: two to three weeks
mapping how your business actually runs, a scored shortlist of what AI can pay for, and a
90-day plan. Nothing gets built until the map says it is worth building. The discipline comes
from twelve years running lean process improvement in enterprise financial services and
infrastructure, applied now to AI adoption for local businesses. Fixed fees, no hourly
billing, no surprise scope. Serving Fort Worth and the greater DFW area.

**Email signature line** (104 chars):
Caleb Bolden | AI consultant, Vora Technologies | I map your process before I touch AI |
calebbolden.com

**Why this option:** Leads with the credential that's hardest to fake, twelve years of lean
process work, and lets AI be the second sentence instead of the first. The tradeoff is that
"process improvement" is a less immediately AI-shaped phrase, so it may need one more beat in
conversation before a prospect connects it to "and now you build AI systems."

## Option 2: Risk and assurance first

**Positioning statement:**
I help small business owners in DFW adopt AI without the risk of it going wrong quietly, by
building every system with gates, supervision, and a monthly check against real numbers.

**LinkedIn headline** (176 chars):
AI consultant for DFW small businesses. Every system I build is gated, supervised, and checked
monthly, so AI adoption never becomes a risk you can't see. Audits start at $750.

**Google Business Profile description** (614 chars):
I help small business owners in Fort Worth and the DFW area adopt AI without the risk of it
going wrong quietly. Every system I build is gated so it cannot take a risky action without
your approval, supervised so it stops and hands off to a person when it hits something it
does not recognize, and checked every month against a standard we set together, with a real
report. The engagement starts with a paid audit: I map your actual workflow, score where AI
genuinely pays off, and hand you a 90-day plan, whether or not I build anything. Fixed fees,
no hourly billing. Serving Fort Worth and the greater DFW area.

**Email signature line** (94 chars):
Caleb Bolden | AI consultant, Vora Technologies | Gated, supervised, checked | calebbolden.com

**Why this option:** Leads with the exact language `/method` already uses for its three
guarantees (gated, supervised, checked) and speaks directly to the AI-skeptical owner
objection the sales research flags as the most common one (job-loss fear, "we tried a chatbot
and it failed"). The tradeoff is that it positions against a fear before establishing the
upside, which can read as defensive if the opening line isn't paired with a concrete result.

---

## Option 3: Audit-before-build, anti-hype

**Positioning statement:**
I help small business owners in DFW cut through AI hype by running a paid audit that tells
them exactly what's worth automating, and refusing to build anything the audit doesn't
justify.

**LinkedIn headline** (153 chars):
AI consultant for DFW small business owners tired of AI hype. I run a paid audit first and
won't build anything it doesn't justify. Audits start at $750.

**Google Business Profile description** (612 chars):
I help small business owners in Fort Worth and the DFW area cut through AI hype and find out
what is actually worth automating. The engagement starts with a paid audit, not a sales pitch:
two to three weeks mapping your real workflow, a scored shortlist of AI opportunities ranked
by payoff and risk, and a 90-day plan. If the audit says a step should be eliminated instead
of automated, I say so. If AI is not the answer, I say that too. You keep the map, the
shortlist, and the plan whether or not I build anything. Fixed fees, no hourly billing, no
surprise scope. Serving Fort Worth and the greater DFW area.

**Email signature line** (105 chars):
Caleb Bolden | AI consultant, Vora Technologies | Audit first, build only what earns it |
calebbolden.com

**Why this option:** Positions against a category ("AI hype," "AI agencies that sell you a
chatbot") rather than a named competitor, which the pricing research flags as a stronger
consulting-positioning move than feature comparison. The tradeoff is that "anti-hype" framing
needs the audit's refusal list (`/method`'s "what I won't do" section) doing real work behind
it, or the claim reads as a slogan rather than a practice.

---

## Recommendation

Option 2 (risk and assurance first). It's the only one of the three that a competitor selling
generic "AI agency" services can't say verbatim, because it depends on the gate/supervise/
check architecture actually being built into the systems, not just claimed in copy. It also
maps directly onto the single most-cited sales objection in the research (fear of AI going
wrong or replacing jobs), so it does double duty as both a differentiator and an objection
pre-answer. Option 1 is the safer, more credential-forward fallback if Option 2 tests as too
defensive in early conversations. Option 3 is the highest-risk, highest-reward choice: it
reads sharpest on a landing page but commits hardest to a "we say no" practice that has to
show up in every sales call, not just the marketing copy.
