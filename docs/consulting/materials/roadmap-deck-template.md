# Roadmap deck template

Template for the final deliverable of the Process & AI Readiness Audit. Built per client in Google Slides from this structure. 12 to 14 slides, presented in person or on a call with the owner.

## Deck-wide rules

- One idea per slide. If a slide needs two takeaways, it's two slides.
- Every number on every slide comes from this client's business. No industry benchmarks standing in for their numbers, no "companies like yours typically see."
- No stock photography, no icons of robots or glowing brains, no AI buzzwords in slide titles. If a slide title could describe any AI vendor's generic pitch, rewrite it.
- The whole deck should run in 25 minutes at a normal talking pace, including the owner's questions. If a walkthrough runs long in practice, cut a slide rather than rush all of them.
- Slide titles state the finding, not the topic. "Job scheduling eats 6 hours a week" beats "Scheduling process."

---

## Slide 1: Title and recap

**Purpose:** Open by reminding the owner exactly what they paid for, so the rest of the deck reads as delivery against a promise, not a sales pitch.

**Content:** Business name, engagement name ("Process & AI Readiness Audit"), dates covered, Caleb's name and Vora Technologies. One line restating the scope in the client's own words from the kickoff.

**Talk track:** "Three weeks ago you asked us to look at where time and money are leaking in the business, and figure out where AI actually helps versus where it doesn't. That's what this is."

---

## Slide 2: How we did it

**Purpose:** Establish credibility for the findings by showing the method was disciplined, not a guess dressed up in a slide deck.

**Content:** Three phases in plain words: Discover (who we talked to, how many interviews, how many hours), Map (what we walked through and observed), Prioritize (how we scored and ranked what we found). Keep each phase to one line, no methodology jargon.

**Talk track:** "We didn't start with a tool in mind. We started by talking to your team, then we mapped how work actually moves through the shop, then we ranked what we found by how much it costs you and how hard it is to fix."

---

## Slide 3: The process we mapped

**Purpose:** Set the scope so the owner isn't confused later about why some other process wasn't covered.

**Content:** One sentence naming the specific process examined (for example: "From the moment a customer calls for service to the moment the invoice is paid"). Name the start point and end point explicitly.

**Talk track:** "We focused on one thing end to end rather than skimming the whole business. This is the path we followed."

---

## Slide 4: What the process looks like today

**Purpose:** Show the client their own workflow laid out, often the first time anyone has drawn it as a full sequence rather than a set of separate jobs.

**Content:** Photo or clean excerpt of the annotated value stream map from the Map phase. If the full map is too dense to read on a slide, crop to the section with the most flagged waste and note that the full map is in the appendix.

**Talk track:** "This is what we saw, step by step, with the delays and handoffs marked where we found them."

---

## Slide 5: What the map showed

**Purpose:** Translate the map into numbers the owner feels, hours and dollars, not process language.

**Content:** Two or three specific bottlenecks from the map, each with an estimated hours-per-week cost and a dollar figure (using a loaded labor rate or lost-revenue estimate agreed on during Discover). Example: "Rescheduling missed appointments: roughly 5 hours a week across two staff, about $650 a week."

**Talk track:** "Here's what those delays actually cost, in hours your team is spending and in dollars, every week, not just once."

---

## Slide 6: What your team told us

**Purpose:** Ground the findings in the client's own people, which builds more buy-in than any chart, and shows the owner things they may not hear directly from staff.

**Content:** Three to four anonymized quotes from interviews, attributed by role only ("front desk," "lead technician") not name. Pick quotes that are specific and concrete, not generic complaints.

**Talk track:** "This isn't just our read on it. Here's what your own people said when we asked them where the friction is."

---

## Slide 7: Readiness snapshot

**Purpose:** Give the owner an honest read on whether the business is set up to actually use new tools well, before recommending any.

**Content:** Four dimension scores from the AI Readiness Assessment (data, technology, people and culture, governance), each with one plain sentence explaining what drove the score. Use the band language from the assessment (not ready yet / ready with conditions / ready), not raw averages alone.

**Talk track:** "Before we talk about what to build, here's where the business stands today on the things that make a new tool succeed or fail. This tells you what to shore up first."

---

## Slide 8: The opportunity list

**Purpose:** Show the full backlog so the owner sees the recommendation in slide 11 was chosen from a real set of options, not the only idea anyone had.

**Content:** Scored backlog table: opportunity name, estimated hours or dollars saved per week, effort to implement, and the combined priority score. Order by score, highest first. Keep to one screen, seven to ten rows max, cut anything too small to matter.

**Talk track:** "This is everything we found worth considering, ranked by impact against effort. A few of these are quick, a few are bigger projects."

---

## Slide 9: What we're not recommending, and why

**Purpose:** This is the slide that earns trust. Recommending against something the owner expected to hear pitched proves the audit wasn't a setup to sell a tool.

**Content:** One or two specific items from the opportunity list (or things the owner asked about directly) that are explicitly not being recommended right now, with the concrete reason: too risky given the readiness score, too small to justify the cost, depends on a system that isn't stable enough yet, or the human judgment involved isn't replaceable.

**Talk track:** "You asked us about automating [X] and we're telling you not to, yet. Here's why, and what would have to change first."

---

## Slide 10: The sequence

**Purpose:** Turn the backlog into a timeline so the owner sees a plan, not just a list.

**Content:** Three columns or bands: next 30 days (quick wins, usually process fixes with no new software), next 90 days (the pilot), and later (items that depend on the pilot's outcome or on readiness gaps closing first).

**Talk track:** "Here's the order we'd tackle this in. Some of it costs nothing and starts this month. The bigger piece is the pilot we'll walk through next."

---

## Slide 11: The pilot recommendation

**Purpose:** Name the one thing being proposed, clearly enough that the owner could describe it back in one sentence after the meeting.

**Content:** The single pilot candidate: what it does in plain language, the specific success metric it will be judged on, and a preview of the stop or scale decision criteria (what result triggers rolling it out further, what result triggers stopping it).

**Talk track:** "Out of everything on that list, this is the one we'd start with. Here's exactly what it does, and here's how we'll know in 90 days if it worked."

---

## Slide 12: What it costs and what it returns

**Purpose:** Make the financial case simply enough that the owner can do the math themselves, which is more persuasive than a number they have to take on faith.

**Content:** Simple ROI math shown as addition and subtraction, not a formula: pilot cost, estimated weekly or monthly savings from slide 5's numbers, and the plain payback period ("pays for itself in about 7 weeks"). Avoid multi-year projections, they invite skepticism, stick to the horizon the owner can picture.

**Talk track:** "Here's the actual math. Not a model, just what it costs versus what it saves based on the numbers we already showed you."

---

## Slide 13: Next step

**Purpose:** End on one clear decision, not a menu, so the meeting produces an answer instead of a "let me think about it" that goes nowhere.

**Content:** The single next step and the single decision being asked of the owner right now (approve the pilot, schedule a kickoff date, or similar). Include a date if one has been proposed.

**Talk track:** "So the decision in front of you today is just this: do you want to move forward with the pilot we just walked through, starting [date]?"

---

## Optional slide 14: Appendix

**Purpose:** Hold anything referenced but not fully shown on its own slide, so the main deck stays lean.

**Content:** Full-resolution value stream map, full interview list (roles and dates, no names if anonymity was promised), full opportunity backlog if slide 8 was trimmed.

**Talk track:** Not presented live unless the owner asks a question that needs it. "I've got the full map and backlog in the appendix if you want to dig into any of this further."
