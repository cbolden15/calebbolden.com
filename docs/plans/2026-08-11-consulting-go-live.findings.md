# Consulting go-live plan hardening findings

## Executive summary

- The plan is not launch-ready under its own definition because the attorney and insurance paths can still prevent signing a client on August 22.
- The calendar is shifted by one weekday throughout, and the latest stated attorney turnaround misses the August 18-20 integration window.
- The stated $1,275-$1,825 committed budget is mis-added at the high end, while optimistic legal-page and GL assumptions leave only $151 in the true worst case.
- The 15-20 Caleb-hour estimate is not supported by the workstream tables; rows naming Caleb total 24.75-33.25 hours before several launch tasks and review time.
- Core operating gaps remain: professional correspondence, W-9 and invoice readiness, business phone coverage, launch-weekend lead handling, GBP fallback, and written founding-offer terms.

## BLOCKER

- [B-01] [Plan section: "The one thing that cannot wait" and Workstreams A/D, lines 23, 60-62, 101, 115, 119: "Turnaround is 3-7 business days" and "after attorney review lands"] [Claim/defect: The plan engages the attorney on August 12 or 13 while the SOW is only starting to be drafted, yet assumes reviewed MSA/SOW language is back and loaded into Anvil during August 18-20. The legal research says "attorney turnaround is typically 3-7 business days" (research-legal-finance.md:33). From Wednesday August 12, seven business days lands Friday August 21; from Thursday August 13, it lands Monday August 24. A later-added SOW or revision round can reset the clock.] [Failure scenario: The attorney either reviews only the MSA, charges for a second scope, or returns the complete packet after launch; Anvil then contains an unreviewed SOW or no usable contract packet.] [Suggested fix: Finish and send the MSA, SOW, payment terms, and three legal questions together on August 12 under a written delivery-by-August-20 commitment that includes one revision round; otherwise make "no signatures" an explicit launch limitation.]

- [B-02] [Plan section: Opening and Risks, lines 3, 123, 132-133: "able to take a client immediately" versus "launch anyway" and "no contract goes out until it lands"] [Claim/defect: The plan uses two incompatible definitions of launch readiness. It promises immediate client intake but accepts launching without the reviewed contract or active insurance required for a signature.] [Failure scenario: A referral says yes on launch weekend, but Caleb must refuse the deposit, improvise paper, or breach the plan's own contract and insurance guards.] [Suggested fix: Add a binary August 20 go-live gate: reviewed MSA/SOW plus active binder/COI means "client-ready"; if either is missing, launch only as a waitlist/booking announcement and state that paid work cannot start yet.]

- [B-03] [Plan section: Clock starters, Budget, Workstream A, and Risks, lines 22, 35-36, 61, 132: "Bind before launch" and "$600" E&O] [Claim/defect: The plan budgets and schedules quote shopping but has no underwriting turnaround, coverage-scope check, binder/COI deadline, or fallback. It also does not require disclosure that the named insured, Vora Technologies LLC, may operate AI product/SaaS businesses as well as consulting. The legal research warns that broader tech E&O can cost "$1,200-$5,000/year at higher revenue/risk profiles" (research-legal-finance.md:46).] [Failure scenario: A carrier asks about AI products, data handling, or shared LLC revenue, excludes part of the operation, or quotes above budget; the MSA's $1M/$2M promise remains unsatisfied and no client can safely sign.] [Suggested fix: Submit complete operations disclosures to all carriers on August 12, require written confirmation of covered consulting and product activities plus binder/COI by August 20, and pre-agree with counsel on contract language if the required limits cannot bind.]
